# Free multi-view bike fitting application

## Intent and agreed direction
Provide the full self-guided fitting workflow at no monetary cost: rider intake, side/front/rear movement analysis, explained adjustments, repeat recordings, and ride feedback. Primary use is on a PC. Phone recordings are the likely common input, not a requirement; accept footage from phones, cameras, webcams, and other sources. Guided comfort and symptom questions are part of the experience.

## Approach
Use a local browser application. Compared with a Python desktop application this reduces installation and distribution work, while allowing local video analysis. A desktop application could support heavier models and broader decoding but adds maintenance. A hosted analysis service would introduce recurring costs and upload requirements, contrary to the zero-cost goal. The user accepted the PC browser direction with general video imports.

## Complete user journey
1. Create a local rider and bike profile: discipline, goals, experience, current discomfort and when it occurs, relevant movement limitations, current bike/contact-point measurements (always metric: millimetres and degrees), and available adjustment tools. Unknown values remain unknown.
2. Save the baseline with photos or notes, saddle height/setback/tilt and cockpit settings where measurable. Explain measurement conventions consistently.
3. Import recordings labeled left side, right side, front, or rear. Require at least one side plus front and rear for a complete assessment; partial input produces a clearly incomplete assessment. Offer generic recording instructions with phone-specific tips, and optional webcam capture where supported. One camera moved between views is sufficient.
4. Check framing, orientation, visible landmarks, duration, motion blur and tracking confidence. Ask for a better recording where evidence is inadequate. Expose overlays and landmark corrections for review.
5. Analyze repeated pedal cycles: side joint-angle ranges and torso posture; front/rear lateral knee trajectories, shoulder/pelvic movement and visible asymmetry. Report image-plane measurements with uncertainty. Independent clips are separate observations, not synchronized 3D reconstruction. Do not infer forces, saddle pressure, anatomical diagnoses or true 3D rotations from these views.
6. Combine measurements with intake and rider feedback into prioritized, explainable adjustment experiments. Show the observation, alternative explanations, evidence/source, confidence, prerequisites, exact action when justified, and what to recheck. Do not turn an uncertain angle into a fabricated millimeter prescription. Bike adjustments must respect user-supplied or manufacturer limits; unverified limits require checking before action.
7. Apply one change at a time, record it, recheck video, and collect comfort feedback. Support retaining or undoing each change. Persistent or concerning symptoms and unresolved uncertainty must result in an appropriate referral rather than escalating speculative adjustments.
8. Record ride duration, intensity and comfort after a trial; compare with baseline and decide whether to retain, revert or investigate further. Export a readable report and portable session data.

## Architecture and implementation choices
Builder proposal: TypeScript browser UI, a local pose-estimation model in a worker, deterministic geometry and versioned fitting rules, IndexedDB for session metadata, and explicit file selection for video. No paid API or account required. Models and runtime assets should be bundled or cached for offline operation after setup. Choose exact libraries after checking their current APIs and redistribution licenses. Use an open source license compatible with dependencies; MIT is preferred for original code if compatible.

Store profile, measurements, recommendations, corrections, rules version and adjustment history locally. Avoid silently storing large videos; offer explicit retention or reattachment with clear deletion and export controls. Explain when browser storage can be cleared. Imported files remain on the device during analysis.

## Video compatibility
Accept video from any source. Inspect actual browser decoding support instead of promising every extension. Handle rotation metadata, landscape/portrait, variable frame rates, timestamps and long clips with trimming and sampling. Support ordinary MP4/WebM where decodable. For unsupported HEVC or other codecs, show a precise explanation and free local conversion instructions; investigate a bundled local conversion option without making it a prerequisite for the entire application. Do not silently upload for conversion. Correct angle calculations for video aspect ratio and display transforms. Use scale calibration only when the reference and measurement lie in a suitable plane.

## Recommendation quality
The fit must explicitly account for problems that bike position cannot resolve. Do not assume that discomfort is caused by fit, or assert that most discomfort is fixable through fit. Intake and follow-up must consider onset, symptoms during and outside cycling, recent training changes, equipment/contact-point issues, and response to prior adjustments. These are context for decisions, not automated diagnoses. Provide distinct outcomes: a supported fit experiment, insufficient evidence requiring further information, an equipment or training factor to investigate, or professional assessment. A person can have more than one contributing factor.

Build a source-backed decision table for when to stop fitting and seek assessment, with source-specific urgency rather than one generic referral message. Research and verify the actual conditions and wording before implementing those rules. Worsening symptoms or repeated unsuccessful adjustments must trigger reassessment instead of an endless series of fit changes. Show what the app observed, what it cannot determine, why an adjustment may not help, and the next appropriate step. Test these pathways alongside successful fitting cases; successful completion may mean recommending no bike change.

The intended product covers the entire process; expert-equivalent accuracy is an evaluation target, not an established claim. Build an auditable evidence table from primary research and fitting guidance, preserving limitations and population context. Separate measured observations from hypotheses and experiments. Fixed target angles cannot override discomfort or serve as diagnoses. Any automated clinical escalation wording needs reviewed sources. No fabricated demo measurements in real reports.

## Verification and completion criteria
The implementation must run locally and complete intake, multi-view import, actual video tracking, measurement review, recommendations, adjustment logging, reanalysis, ride feedback and export. Verify geometry using known-coordinate fixtures, confidence rejection and missing-view behavior, persisted history and undo, and representative browser/video-format behavior. Test in the rendered application. Clearly label synthetic fixtures and sample sessions. Real-world accuracy requires consented recordings with reference measurements and expert-reviewed cases; the initial release cannot claim this validation unless it actually exists. Document any missing validation separately from functional completeness.

## Scope and builder discretion
Support road, gravel, mountain and stationary cycling context, with recommendations limited to evidence applicable to each. Do not substitute one discipline's target ranges for another. No subscription, cloud account, payment system or public deployment is required. Public publishing requires a separate user instruction. Builder may choose UI styling, project name, dependency details and folder structure. Keep implementation separate from the existing pwn.college material. Before implementation, produce a sequenced plan that addresses research, tracking feasibility, video compatibility, recommendations, workflow and verification.
